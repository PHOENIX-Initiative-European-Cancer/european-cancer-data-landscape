# Non-FHIR landscape overview

Consolidation of the five regional non-FHIR landscape reports (dach-benelux-landscape.md, south-landscape.md, nordic-baltic-landscape.md, central-east-landscape.md, islands-uk-landscape.md), surveyed 2026-11-18 for the ECCDM editors; "n/f" = not found in the reports, source URLs are in the regional files. France has no regional landscape report yet and is marked accordingly.

## E. Patterns

1. **Near-complete national coverage is the norm, with four clear gaps.** The reports state nationwide or 100% coverage (by law or per the ECIS list) for Germany, Austria, Switzerland (since 2020), Netherlands, Belgium, Portugal, UK, Ireland, Cyprus, Malta, Denmark, Sweden, Norway (98.6%), Finland, Iceland (>99%), Estonia, Lithuania, Poland, Czechia, Slovakia, Hungary, Slovenia, Croatia and Bulgaria. Coverage is partial in Italy (AIRTUM about 80–84%, national registry fed by regions), Spain (REDECAN about 25%), Romania (regional registries, new national registry with unknown operational status) and Greece (no historic population registry, national registry only since Feb 2025). Latvia sits in between: cancer data live in a general disease register while a dedicated registry is being built. A common model must therefore accept registry-derived national cohorts from most countries but regional samples from Italy, Spain and Romania.

2. **ECIS feeding is confirmed for 16 of 31 countries and unverified for the others.** Explicit ECIS contribution is recorded for Netherlands, Ireland, Cyprus, Denmark, Sweden, Norway, Finland, Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania, Poland, Czechia, Slovakia, Slovenia, Croatia and Bulgaria; Hungary contributes only its paediatric registry, Romania only regional inputs, Malta via projections. For Germany, Austria, Switzerland, Belgium, Luxembourg, Italy, Spain, Portugal, Greece and Iceland the reports could not verify a contribution, and the UK is outside the EU27 estimates. Greece's new IDIKA registry states ECIS interoperability as a design goal, which is the only case where a registry build explicitly targets ECIS.

3. **Submission formats are documented in roughly half the countries, and only a handful are machine-readable schemas.** National XML or structured interfaces exist in Germany (oBDS XML 3.0.5/3.1.0), UK (COSD XML, SACT, RTDS), Czechia (DASTA), Slovakia (ISZI XML), Norway (KREMT, Svar XML), Denmark (LPR3, MEDRPT EDI), Poland (e-KRN+ web application) and Belgium (healthdata.be CSV); Italy uses AIRTUM flat files, Sweden INCA forms, Netherlands a per-diagnosis itemset with FHIR extraction, Switzerland a FHIR exchange format alongside PDF. Formats are n/f for Luxembourg, Spain, Portugal, Greece, Cyprus, Malta, Finland (current spec), Iceland, Slovenia, Hungary (by agreement), Romania and Bulgaria. Where editions are stated, TNM 8 dominates (Austria, Netherlands, Italy, UK, Poland, Czechia, Slovakia); Germany already carries TNM 9 in oBDS 3.0.5, Czechia and Slovakia reference TNM 9 for 2026–2027, Ireland still reports UICC TNM 7. ICD-O-3 (mostly revision 2 / 3.2) is universal wherever stated.

4. **Clinical and quality registries are a Nordic, Dutch, UK and German strength and largely absent in the south and east.** Denmark (25 DMCG databases on RKKP), Sweden (about 30 INCA quality registers plus IPÖ), Norway (12 quality registries), UK (ten NATCAN audits), Netherlands (PALGA), Germany (clinical registration merged into the state registries) and Belgium (MOC forms) have systematic clinical datasets beyond incidence. Poland (organ registries on e-KRN+), Czechia (centre-treatment interface) and Slovenia (OI clinical registries) are building them; Italy, Spain, Portugal, Greece, Cyprus, Malta, Ireland, Iceland, Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania, Slovakia, Croatia, Romania and Bulgaria report none at national level. Treatment and outcome elements of a common model will have real national source data mainly in the first group.

5. **CDA and HL7 v3 dominate national EHR exchange; openEHR is established in a small cluster; DASTA and bespoke XML persist.** CDA or HL7 v3 is the national document standard in Austria (ELGA), Switzerland (EPD), Italy (FSE 2.0), Spain (HCDSNS), Finland (Kanta), Estonia (ENHIS), Latvia (e-veselība), Poland (PIK CDA), Croatia (CEZIH), Romania (DES) and Hungary (EESZT). openEHR carries cancer-relevant content in Sweden (INCA CDR, pathology archetypes, prostate MDT), Norway (MDT work), Slovenia (CRPP and OI Ljubljana via Better), Malta (national Better platform) and Lithuania (pilot). Czechia runs DASTA nationally, Slovakia mandates ISO 13606, Belgium keeps KMEHR XML, Bulgaria exposes NHIS XML/XSD APIs, and Denmark and Norway use EDI/XML messaging. Structured pathology outside FHIR is documented in Netherlands (PALGA), UK (RCPath/ICCR), Sweden (KVAST), Norway (NORPAT), Denmark (Patobank SNOMED), Poland (CAP synoptic), Italy (Emilia-Romagna CDA) and Czechia (DASTA pathology report); MDT software is named only for UK, Ireland, Germany, Italy, Sweden and Norway.

6. **OMOP is established where EHDEN clusters exist, and several cancer registries are already in OMOP.** Large EHDEN footprints are reported for Spain (27), Italy (26), UK (17–22), Belgium (14), Portugal (11), Finland (FinOMOP plus seven) and Greece, Hungary, Croatia and Switzerland (five each); cancer registries converted or converting to OMOP are the Dutch NKR, Belgian BCR (breast/lung), Luxembourg RNC, Norwegian Cancer Registry and the Italian Basilicata registry, with Edinburgh working on the OMOP Oncology Module. No EHDEN partner is listed for Poland, Slovakia, Slovenia, Romania, Cyprus, Malta, Latvia or Iceland, and Germany's oncology OMOP work is n/f (the DKTK/BZKF platform builds on MII FHIR). An ECCDM-to-OMOP mapping therefore has immediate consumers in the west and south but few in Central and Eastern Europe.

7. **Health Data Access Bodies are operational in a few countries and in project phase in most.** Operating bodies are named for Germany (FDZ Gesundheit, Oct 2025), Belgium (Health Data Agency, 2024), Finland (Findata), Denmark (Sundhedsdatastyrelsen), Norway (Helsedataservice) and Czechia (ÚZIS/NZIS); preparatory EU4Health projects exist in Netherlands (HDAB-NL), Luxembourg (HDAB-LU), Spain (HDAB-ES), Portugal (HealthData@PT), Greece (GR-HDAB), Malta (MHDAB), Slovenia (SI-SUD) and Italy (designation pending, Agenas). HDAB status is n/f for Poland, Slovakia, Hungary, Croatia, Romania, Bulgaria, Latvia and Austria; the UK runs its own SDE network outside EHDS and Ireland expects EHDS to apply from 2029. Only Germany's FDZ and the Nordic bodies explicitly offer cancer registry linkage today.

8. **Coverage caveats in this consolidation.** France was researched in a dedicated deep dive (france-landscape.md); its rows were filled from that report after the consolidation. Luxembourg appears in both the DACH-Benelux and South reports; the DACH-Benelux version was preferred and South facts (RNC established 2013, grand-ducal regulation, LNS as source) were merged where they did not conflict. Cyprus and Malta appear in both the South and Islands-UK reports; the Islands-UK version was preferred. Disagreements: Malta's ENCR status (full member per South, associate member per Islands-UK), Malta HDAB (existing with catalogue per South, being set up per Islands-UK), Cyprus EUCAIM partner (German Oncology Center per South, n/f per Islands-UK) and Cyprus HDAB (under NeHA per South, n/f per Islands-UK). OECI accreditation lists for Germany, Italy and Greece are n/f in the reports, so the CCC column understates those countries.
